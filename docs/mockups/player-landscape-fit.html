Every control, sideways

The shipped player, unchanged in what it offers, folded so it fits a phone held sideways. Nothing hides. Nothing moves behind a menu. Speed, both loop markers, volume, mute, hand colours and Listen mode all stay on screen, because a player you have to go looking through is no use for careful practice.

The three things that do change — none of them a control

  1. Title and composer share one line instead of stacking. Same words, one row shorter: Air — BWV Anh. 131 · BACH · MUTOPIA CATALOG, truncating from the right.
  2. The ← → SKIP 5 SECONDS hint drops below 620px. It describes two keys on a hardware keyboard, on a device that does not have one. The shortcuts themselves keep working for anyone with a keyboard attached.
  3. The RIGHT / LEFT text legend drops — as it already does below 1024px today. The coloured swatches on the hand-colour button say the same thing in a quarter of the width.

Everything else is the shipped player at a smaller scale. Above 620px tall not a single pixel moves, so the laptop and the iPad are untouched.

The scroll that reveals nothing

Separate from the layout, and the thing that makes the player feel broken rather than cramped. The stylesheet gives the page a minimum height of 100% while the player is sized to 100dvh. On a phone those are different numbers: 100% measures the screen with the browser toolbars hidden, 100dvh measures it as it is right now. The difference — exactly one toolbar — becomes scrollable empty space. Two-line fix, and it goes first.

On an iPhone, there is no fullscreen button — install it instead

Safari on iPhone has never reliably offered the fullscreen API for anything but a video, so a fullscreen button would do nothing there. The app is already built as an installable web app that opens with no browser chrome at all, which is better than fullscreen because it is permanent and needs no button.

To install it on an iPhone:

  1. Open piano-practice-player.wowanirudh.workers.dev in Safari — this does not work in Chrome on iPhone.
  2. Tap the Share button: the square with an arrow pointing up, in the bar at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Scroll the grey list down to Add to Home Screen and tap it.
  4. Tap Add, top right.
  5. Open it from the new icon on the Home Screen, not from Safari. There should be no address bar and no toolbar — turn the phone sideways and the whole screen is the player.

A fullscreen button still ships for the browsers that do support it — desktop, Android Chrome, iPad — and simply does not render where the browser cannot honour it. That is why the layout above has to work on its own.

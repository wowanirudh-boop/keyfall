Can you see the keys?

The shipped keyboard puts a white key at #151821 next to a black key at #0C0E11. That is a contrast ratio of 1.09:1. Anything below 3:1 fails WCAG for a control you have to identify; 1.09:1 means the two key types are, measurably, the same colour. Below are the ways out, rendered as real keyboards, with the three rejected ones kept so the reasoning stays checkable. Judge them on the phone, not the laptop — that is where the problem is.

dark room

Drag the slider. It adds a constant luminance floor to every pixel, which is exactly what a lit room does to a screen through reflection. This is the reason the keyboard looks worse on your phone than on the laptop: a phone is used in far more light, and the darker two colours are, the faster room light collapses the difference between them.

Why ivory looked wrong, and why white does not

Two things need contrast, and on one flat key face they pull in opposite directions:

  • Identification — a white key against a black key. Wants the white key lighter.
  • State — a lit key, painted in the hand colour #4CC2FF, against an unlit one. Wants the white key darker, because the hand colour is itself a light blue.

They cross at roughly #5C6572 — option 2 — where both land near 3:1 and neither is comfortable. Everything between there and white is the uncanny middle, and ivory sits in the worst of it: #C6CBD4 is 1.23:1 against the lit key, meaning the keyboard and the keys being played are within a hair of the same lightness. That is the flat, plasticky look — it is not taste, it is a measurement.

Going further is what fixes it. At #F0F2F6 the key face is clearly above the hand colour rather than level with it, so identification climbs to 17.35:1 and the lit key separates by 1.79:1 instead of 1.23:1. Both numbers improve at once, which is the sign you have left the trade-off rather than split it. The dark ring then finishes the job the luminance cannot.

Why the phone is worse than the laptop

  • Room light, above all. Contrast between two near-blacks collapses toward 1.00:1 as reflected light raises the screen's black floor. The shipped pair goes from 1.09:1 in a dark room to 1.02:1 in daylight. A laptop at a desk sits in far less light than a phone on a music stand.
  • Smaller keys. At 844px landscape a white key is 16.2px wide; the only thing separating two white keys is a 1px border at 1.23:1. Narrower keys make that separator a smaller share of what you see.
  • Black crush. Phone OLED panels and their ambient-light compensation routinely flatten everything below about 4% luminance. Both key colours live there — 9 code values apart out of 255.

What this does not change

The waterfall, the stage, the transport and every other surface stay exactly as designed. Only the keyboard moves. A real piano in a dim room still shows light keys — the instrument is lit, the room is not, and that is the picture all three options are reaching for to different degrees.
